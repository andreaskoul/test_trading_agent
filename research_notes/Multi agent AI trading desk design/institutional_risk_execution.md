# Institutional Portfolio Construction, Risk, Execution and Model Governance for a Small Automated Weekly News-Driven Long-Short Book

Context: S&P 500 universe, ~20 names, weekly rebalance, LLM analyst scores, beta-hedged with SPY, paper trading on Alpaca. Research date: 2026-09-28. Notes flag which items were verified by fetching the primary source vs. taken from search listings / standard textbook knowledge.

## 1. Signal-to-portfolio: scores to weights, combination, turnover, holding period / news-alpha decay

### Takeaway
Institutional practice converts scores to alphas with the Grinold rule alpha = IC x residual vol x z-score (so a discrete LLM score must first be standardised cross-sectionally and shrunk by a realistic IC of ~0.02-0.05), then sizes via a risk model or, for a 20-name book, simple inverse-residual-vol weights with caps. News alpha has two regimes: daily-sentiment effects reverse within 1-2 days, while weekly-aggregated / "real information" news drifts for weeks to a quarter and bad news is incorporated slowly -- which supports a weekly holding period but argues against trading on the latest day's headlines alone.

### Cited Findings
- Grinold & Kahn "forecasting rule of thumb": alpha = volatility x IC x score, where score is a standardised (mean 0, std 1) signal; refined alphas should be scaled so their cross-sectional std equals IC x residual vol, and outliers trimmed (typically at +/-3 std) — [Grinold & Kahn, Active Portfolio Management (1999/2000), study notes by Z. Yan](https://people.brandeis.edu/~yanzp/Study%20Notes/Active%20Portfolio%20Management.pdf); [Grinold & Kahn full text](http://cms.dm.uba.ar/academico/materias/2docuat2016/analisis_cuantitativo_en_finanzas/Richard%20Grinold,%20Ronald%20Kahn-Active%20Portfolio%20Management_%20A%20Quantitative%20Approach%20for%20Producing%20Superior%20Returns%20and%20Controlling%20Risk-McGraw-Hill%20(1999).pdf); MSCI Barra and Northfield have practitioner notes on the same conversion ("Converting Scores into Alphas", "Alpha Scaling Revisited", Shah 2007) — [MSCI (link now redirects)](https://app2.msci.com/products/analytics/aegis/PI_Converting_Scores_Into_Alphas.pdf); [Northfield, Shah 2007](https://www.northinfo.com/Documents/247.pdf) (not fetched; titles from search listing).
- Heston & Sinha ("News versus Sentiment", SSRN 2311310; >900,000 Reuters stories; Harvard IV, Loughran-McDonald, Thomson-Reuters neural sentiment): "Daily news predicts stock returns for only 1 to 2 days" with quick reversal, but "weekly news ... predicts stock returns for one quarter"; positive news is incorporated quickly while negative news shows "a long-delayed reaction"; simple dictionary methods capture short, reversing moves while more sophisticated (neural) measures identify larger, more durable effects — [Heston & Sinha, SSRN](https://ssrn.com/abstract=2311310) (abstract verified).
- Chan (2003, JFE 70(2):223-260) "Stock price reaction to news and no-news: drift and reversal after headlines": stocks with public news show momentum/drift (strongest after bad news), while large price moves without news tend to reverse — [Chan 2003, RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v70y2003i2p223-260.html); [working paper PDF](http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2001-05-11/chan.pdf) (full text not retrievable at research time; characterisation from widely cited abstract — treat magnitudes as unverified).
- Tetlock ("All the News That's Fit to Reprint", RFS 2011): markets over-react to stale (repeated) news, with partial reversal of the stale-news reaction in the following week — [Tetlock PDF](http://www.econ.yale.edu/~shiller/behfin/2007-12/tetlock.pdf) (not fetched; from search listing and standard knowledge of the paper).
- Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz: price impact is concave (roughly square-root) in trade size, so turnover cost rises less than linearly but is still the main drag on fast signals — [Trading Costs, 2018/2021 PDF](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/Trading-Cost.pdf) (verified).

### Inferences
- Concrete weight pipeline for a 20-name book: (1) map LLM score s_i (e.g. -2..+2) x confidence c_i into a raw signal; (2) cross-sectionally z-score over the candidate set, winsorise at +/-2.5-3; (3) alpha_i = IC x sigma_resid,i x z_i with IC set conservatively (0.02-0.05) until live evidence exists; (4) weight proportional to alpha_i / sigma_resid,i^2 (the diagonal-risk-model MVO solution), which simplifies to w_i proportional to z_i / sigma_resid,i — i.e. "score over residual vol"; (5) impose caps (e.g. 10% per name of gross) and renormalise each side to target gross; (6) hedge residual beta with SPY. Confidence should enter as shrinkage of the z-score toward 0 (z_i x c_i), not as a separate multiplier after sizing.
- Pure rank weighting (equal-weight top/bottom decile) is robust and a sensible default while IC is unknown; score x confidence without vol scaling will overweight volatile names (which also carry the biggest news reactions).
- Holding period: evidence supports a 1-week hold for signals built on aggregated weekly news and "real information" events, and suggests (a) avoiding trading on price moves that lack news (reversal risk per Chan), (b) expecting the short leg (bad news) to earn drift over longer horizons than the long leg, (c) checking that the LLM is not scoring stale/repeated stories as new (Tetlock). Measure the realised signal decay curve (IC at 1,2,5,10,20 days) on your own data before committing.
- Turnover control: carry over positions unless the new alpha differs by more than the round-trip cost (a "no-trade band"), or blend new target with old weights (e.g. w_new = 0.5 w_target + 0.5 w_old) — both standard; parameterise by backtest.
- Signal combination: if multiple agents score the same name, average z-scores (equal weights) before any learned weighting — equal weights are hard to beat out of sample with short histories.

## 2. Risk: beta estimation, sector/style neutrality, risk models, limits, earnings, drawdown controls

### Takeaway
Use daily-return betas over ~1 year with outlier-robust estimation (Welch's slope-winsorised beta, or at minimum Vasicek/Blume shrinkage toward 1); a raw 60-day OLS beta has a standard error around 0.2 for a typical large cap, which is too noisy to hedge individual names precisely. Keep the book sector-neutral (or cap net sector exposure), monitor style tilts, and add explicit gross/net/name limits, earnings-date rules and a drawdown kill switch.

### Cited Findings
- Welch, "Simply Better Market Betas" (Critical Finance Review 2022, 11:37-64): winsorise each daily stock return to a band around the market return of (1 +/- 3) x r_m (i.e. between -2 x r_m and +4 x r_m), then run OLS; optional exponential age decay of 2/256 per day (half-life ~90 trading days, ~4 months); standard window 252 daily returns, minimum ~20 days (100+ in many tests). Reported out-of-sample RMSE improvements: ~5-10% vs OLS, ~4-5% vs Vasicek, ~30-40% vs Bloomberg/Blume adjusted beta. Daily-frequency betas outperform monthly ones even for forecasting future monthly betas — [Welch 2022 PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.org/forthcoming/papers/welch2022simply.pdf) (verified); code at [ivo-welch.info](https://ivo-welch.info/research/betas/code/).
- Blume adjustment (used by Bloomberg "adjusted beta"): beta_adj = 0.67 x beta_raw + 0.33 x 1.0; Vasicek shrinks toward the cross-sectional mean with weight based on relative estimation variance — [CFI, adjusted beta](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/adjusted-beta/); comparisons in [Hawawini & Vora, INSEAD WP 1981](https://flora.insead.edu/fichiersti_wp/inseadwp1981/81-16.pdf) (not fetched).
- Alpaca margin rules relevant to gross/net: overnight buying power 2x (50% initial margin); short positions in stocks >= $5 require the greater of $5/share or 30% of value — [Alpaca Margin & Short Selling docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling) (verified).
- SR 11-7 expects documented limits, monitoring and escalation for model outputs (see section 4) — [Federal Reserve SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm).

### Inferences
- Beta noise (own calculation): SE(beta) ~ sigma_idio / (sigma_mkt x sqrt(n)). For a large cap with ~25% idiosyncratic and ~16% market annual vol, n=60 days gives SE ~0.20; n=252 gives ~0.10. Hence: use 252-day, slope-winsorised, then shrink ~1/3 toward 1 (or toward a sector-average beta). Hedging at the portfolio level diversifies per-name beta error by roughly 1/sqrt(N), so a 20-name book's aggregate beta error is ~0.05-0.1 per unit of gross leg — acceptable; target |ex-ante net beta| < 0.1.
- Hedge-ratio practice: compute dollar beta of longs minus shorts; SPY hedge = -(net dollar beta). Rebalance the hedge weekly with the book, and intra-week only if realised net beta drifts beyond a band (e.g. +/-0.15).
- Sector neutrality: with 20 names, full GICS-sector neutrality is often infeasible; practical rule is |net sector exposure| <= 10-15% of gross per GICS sector, or pair longs/shorts within sector. News signals cluster by sector (industry news days), so unconstrained books silently become sector bets.
- Style exposures to monitor weekly (regress book returns or compute holdings-weighted exposures): momentum (12-1), short-term reversal (1-month), size, value, low-vol/beta. News-driven long-short books are structurally exposed to short-term reversal/momentum (they buy recent good-news winners) — report this exposure explicitly. A statistical risk model (PCA on 1-2 years of daily residual returns, 5-15 factors) is a cheap substitute for Barra at this scale.
- Suggested limits for a ~20-name book: gross <= 2x equity (Alpaca overnight cap), net dollar within +/-10% before hedge, net beta within +/-0.1 after hedge; single name <= 10% of gross (<= 5% for shorts, since short losses are unbounded); no more than 30% of gross in one sector; minimum ADV screen (S&P 500 names generally satisfy).
- Earnings: either exclude names with earnings inside the holding week, or halve their size; earnings-day moves dominate weekly idiosyncratic variance and are not what a news-reading model is forecasting unless explicitly designed to.
- Drawdown controls: (a) soft stop — cut gross by 50% if trailing drawdown exceeds ~2x expected monthly vol; (b) hard kill switch — flatten and require human sign-off if drawdown exceeds ~3-4x monthly vol, if realised beta/net exposure breaches limits, if data feeds/LLM outputs fail validation, or if orders exceed size sanity checks. Per-name stop-losses are generally avoided by stat-arb desks as they add turnover and cut mean-reversion winners; prefer portfolio-level controls.

## 3. Execution: MOC vs VWAP/TWAP, cost models, short borrow, implementation shortfall

### Takeaway
For a weekly book in S&P 500 names at small size, market-on-close (or limit-on-close) orders are the institutional default: minimal impact, a clean benchmark matching close-to-close backtests, and a liquid auction. Budget ~5-10 bps one-way for realistic institutional costs in large caps (Frazzini-Israel-Moskowitz), measure implementation shortfall vs. the decision price, and assume ETB shorts are free on Alpaca but ~30 bps/yr at institutional GC rates.

### Cited Findings
- Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz ("Trading Costs", 1998-2016, $1.7tn of live AQR executions across 21 developed markets): actual costs are "an order of magnitude smaller" than prior academic estimates; mean market impact for large caps ~8.9 bps (value-weighted overall sample ~15 bps); impact is concave in trade size (log-log slope ~0.35 vs. 0.5 for square-root); implementation shortfall defined as benchmark (prior-close / model) price return minus actual traded return — [FIM PDF](https://spinup-000d1a-wp-offload-media.s3.amazonaws.com/faculty/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2021/08/Trading-Cost.pdf); [SSRN 3229719](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3229719) (verified).
- NYSE (Aug 22, 2023): for Russell 1000 stocks, orders of ~2.4% of closing-auction volume had immediate impact of ~0.34-0.4x the average daily spread; orders up to ~0.5-1.2% of closing auction volume in the last minutes showed no persistent impact; post-close drift 0.6-1.7x spread for Russell 1000 — [NYSE Data Insights, Closing Auction market impact](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/closing-auction-immediate-market-impact-price-drift-and-transaction-cost-of-trading) (verified).
- Academic benchmark for closing vs. opening auction vs. continuous price impact exists (JFQA) — [Cambridge Core, "Price Impact in Closing Auctions, Opening Auctions, and Continuous Markets"](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846) (not fetched).
- Alpaca: MOC/LOC use time_in_force "cls"; orders submitted between 3:50pm and 7:00pm ET are rejected; after 7:00pm they queue for the next day's close; unfilled auction orders are cancelled. OPG (opening auction) orders are rejected between 9:28am and 7:00pm ET — [Alpaca Orders docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca) (verified).
- Alpaca short selling: 5,000+ easy-to-borrow (ETB) names with zero borrow fee for Trading API users; hard-to-borrow requires a locate and round lots of 100; HTB fee = position value x rate / 360, with weekend holding charged 3 days; no mention of fractional shorts — [Alpaca Margin & Short Selling](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling) (verified).
- S3 Partners (Jan 24, 2024): general-collateral borrow fee ~0.30%/yr covers >80% of US short-interest dollars; only ~4.6-5.5% of short interest carried fees >1%; stocks with fees >=10% were <2% of shorted dollars but >40% of borrow costs — [S3 Partners, US Stock Borrow Fees](https://www.s3partners.com/articles/us-stock-borrow-fees) (verified).
- Paper-trading fills on Alpaca are simulated; third parties note the paper environment omits realistic slippage/queue effects — [TradersPost blog](https://blog.traderspost.io/article/alpaca-paper-trading) (not fetched; treat as unverified).

### Inferences
- Execution recipe: generate targets after Friday's (or chosen day's) close data, submit MOC orders before 3:50pm ET on the rebalance day (build in a 3:40pm hard deadline with retry), SPY hedge also MOC so legs price at the same print. If signals are generated overnight, use OPG orders submitted before 9:28am — but opening auctions are thinner and noisier than the close.
- Cost model for backtests/paper P&L: one-way cost = half-spread (~1-3 bps for S&P 500 names) + impact (negligible at retail size) + a conservative buffer => charge 5 bps one-way (10 bps round trip) per name, 1 bp for SPY; plus 30 bps/yr on short notional to mirror institutional GC even though Alpaca ETB is free. At 100% weekly two-sided turnover this is ~5 bps x 2 legs x 52 ≈ 5%/yr of gross — often larger than the gross alpha, so turnover control matters more than execution algo choice.
- VWAP/TWAP only make sense when orders are a material fraction of volume — not the case here; they also desynchronise from close-to-close backtests.
- Implementation shortfall to log per rebalance: (fill price - decision price) x signed shares, with decision price = price at signal timestamp (and separately vs. prior close), split into delay cost (signal to order) and execution cost (order to fill), plus opportunity cost for unfilled orders. Paper fills should be compared with actual closing-auction prints to detect simulation optimism.
- Only short ETB names; re-check ETB status before each rebalance (lists change) and have a fallback (drop the short and rebalance the other side, or hedge via sector ETF).

## 4. Model governance & MLOps: pre-registration, champion/challenger, shadow periods, drift monitoring, change management, incident response, SR 11-7

### Takeaway
SR 11-7 (Fed/OCC, April 2011) is the reference framework: every model (the LLM scorer counts — qualitative inputs with quantitative outputs are in scope) needs documentation sufficient for an outsider, an inventory entry, independent "effective challenge", validation covering conceptual soundness, ongoing monitoring and outcomes analysis, and at least annual review. For a small automated book this translates into versioned, pre-registered configs, a frozen champion with shadow challengers, and automated monitors with kill-switch escalation.

### Cited Findings
- SR 11-7 definition: a model is "a quantitative method, system, or approach that applies statistical, economic, financial, or mathematical theories, techniques, and assumptions to process input data into quantitative estimates", including approaches with partly qualitative inputs — [Federal Reserve SR 11-7 (Apr 4, 2011)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm) (verified).
- Validation core elements: (1) evaluation of conceptual soundness; (2) ongoing monitoring to confirm the model works as intended and whether changes in "products, exposures, activities, clients, or market conditions" require adjustment; (3) outcomes analysis comparing outputs to actual outcomes, including back-testing on data not used in development — [SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm).
- "Effective challenge": "critical analysis by objective, informed parties that can identify model limitations and produce appropriate changes"; requires incentives, competence and influence — [SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm).
- Maintain an inventory of models "implemented for use, under development for implementation, or recently retired"; documentation detailed enough that "parties unfamiliar with a model" understand its operation, limitations and assumptions; review "at least annually but more frequently if warranted" — [SR 11-7](https://www.federalreserve.gov/supervisionreg/srletters/sr1107.htm).
- Multiple-testing discipline: Bailey & López de Prado argue that the number of trials must be recorded to deflate reported Sharpe ratios (see section 5) — [Deflated Sharpe Ratio, JPM 2014](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf).

### Inferences
- Model inventory for this system: news ingestion/dedup, LLM prompt+model version (scorer), score-to-alpha mapping, beta/risk model, optimiser/constraints, execution module, hedging module. Each gets a version ID; every trade record stores the full version tuple.
- Pre-registration: before a live/paper period, write a dated, git-committed spec: universe, rebalance day/time, prompt hash and model ID, weighting rule, limits, cost assumptions, primary metric (e.g. beta-neutral weekly IR), evaluation horizon and success/failure thresholds. Count every variant tried (feeds the DSR's N).
- LLM-specific risks (governance should treat these as model limitations): model/provider version changes silently altering scores; look-ahead leakage when backtesting an LLM whose training data post-dates the test period (backtests pre-cutoff are contaminated — only post-cutoff or live paper results are clean); non-determinism (fix temperature, log raw outputs, re-score a fixed "canary" set of articles weekly to detect drift).
- Champion/challenger: one champion trades (paper); challengers compute targets on the same data in shadow and are scored with the same cost model. Promotion only after a pre-registered minimum shadow period (e.g. >= 13-26 weeks) and a statistically meaningful difference (see section 5); never promote on a few good weeks.
- Monitoring (weekly, automated): score distribution drift (mean, dispersion, share of extreme scores, PSI vs. trailing 12 weeks), coverage (names scored, articles per name), rank IC realised vs. expected, turnover, realised beta and factor exposures vs. ex-ante, cost/slippage vs. model, P&L vs. vol-implied bands. Breaches trigger alerts; severe breaches trigger the kill switch.
- Change management: any change to prompt, LLM version, universe, weights or limits is a new model version — requires changelog, backtest/shadow comparison, and restarts the track-record clock for statistical evaluation purposes.
- Incident response: runbook for data outage, LLM API failure (default: hold existing positions, do not trade on partial scores), order rejections (e.g. submission after 3:50pm cutoff), unexpected fills, and limit breaches; post-mortem logged per incident.

## 5. Performance evaluation: track-record length, deflated Sharpe, PBO, sequential testing, IR, attribution

### Takeaway
A weekly book's Sharpe is statistically indistinguishable from zero for a long time: at a true annual SR of 1.0 you need roughly 2.7 years of weekly returns for 95% one-sided confidence, and far longer for SR 0.5 — before any adjustment for the number of variants tried. Report PSR/DSR with the recorded trial count, require t-stats around 3 for newly discovered signals, and attribute returns to beta, sectors, styles and residual.

### Cited Findings
- Bailey & López de Prado, "The Deflated Sharpe Ratio" (Journal of Portfolio Management, 2014): the expected maximum Sharpe ratio from N independent trials is E[max SR] ≈ E[SR] + sigma(SR) x [(1-gamma) Phi^-1(1-1/N) + gamma Phi^-1(1-1/(N e))], gamma ≈ 0.5772; the DSR is the Probabilistic Sharpe Ratio evaluated against this inflated threshold, correcting also for skewness, kurtosis and sample length. Worked example: N=88 trials, observed annualised SR 2.5, T=1,250 daily observations (5 years) gives a DSR around the 95% boundary — [Bailey & López de Prado PDF](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf); [SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2460551). Note: the fetch summary reported both "DSR 0.9505" and "only a 90% chance ... greater than zero"; consult the paper for the exact figure.
- PSR = Phi[(SR_hat - SR*) sqrt(T-1) / sqrt(1 - skew x SR_hat + (kurt-1)/4 x SR_hat^2)] and Minimum Track Record Length MinTRL = 1 + [1 - skew x SR_hat + (kurt-1)/4 x SR_hat^2] x (z_alpha / (SR_hat - SR*))^2, with SR in per-period (not annualised) units — [López de Prado, "Deflating the Sharpe Ratio by asking for a Minimum Track Record Length" (slides)](http://boston.qwafafew.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2017/01/Lopez_de_Prado_Sharpe.pdf); [Portfolio Optimizer blog](https://portfoliooptimizer.io/blog/the-probabilistic-sharpe-ratio-hypothesis-testing-and-minimum-track-record-length-for-the-difference-of-sharpe-ratios/) (formulas from standard statement; not re-verified from these pages).
- Harvey, Liu & Zhu ("... and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", RFS 2016, 29(1):5-68): given hundreds of tested factors, a new factor should clear a t-statistic of about 3.0 rather than 2.0 — [RFS / Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/29/1/5/1843824) (from search listing; widely known result).
- Open-source implementations of PSR/MinTRL and FDR/FWER screening: [jsharpe (GitHub)](https://github.com/tschm/jsharpe).

### Inferences
- MinTRL for weekly returns, normal returns, benchmark SR*=0, 95% one-sided (own calculation): annual SR 2.0 -> ~38 weeks (~0.7 yr); SR 1.0 -> ~143 weeks (~2.75 yrs); SR 0.5 -> ~565 weeks (~11 yrs). Fat tails / negative skew lengthen these. Practical implication: a few months of paper trading can only reject catastrophic failure or detect implementation bugs, not confirm alpha.
- Because weekly P&L gives only 52 obs/yr, evaluate the signal on higher-power statistics too: weekly cross-sectional rank IC (many names per week -> more observations; t-stat of mean IC over weeks), hit rate, and long vs. short leg returns separately.
- Sequential testing: pre-register checkpoints (e.g. 13, 26, 52 weeks) with alpha-spending (e.g. O'Brien-Fleming-style tighter early thresholds) or a Sequential Probability Ratio Test on weekly residual returns, to allow early stopping for failure without inflating false positives. (Standard statistical methods; no trading-specific source fetched.)
- Probability of Backtest Overfitting (Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado, Zhu, CSCV method): split history into S blocks, evaluate all configurations on all train/test combinations, and report how often the in-sample best ranks below median out-of-sample; use when choosing among prompt/weighting variants. (Not fetched in this session.)
- Benchmarking: for a beta-hedged book the relevant benchmark is cash (T-bill), so IR ≈ Sharpe of excess return; still report realised beta to SPY and the correlation to standard factor ETFs/returns (momentum, value, size, low-vol, short-term reversal) to show returns are not repackaged factor exposure.
- Attribution each week: total = beta x SPY + hedge P&L + sector + style factors + residual (stock-specific) - costs - borrow; plus long-leg vs. short-leg and signal-bucket (by score / confidence) attribution to check that higher confidence actually delivers higher returns (calibration).
