# Academic literature on intraday execution timing in US equities: the close, the open, and the closing price as a benchmark

Scope note: US equities unless flagged. "Large" in Bogousslavsky & Muravyev means the top NYSE+Nasdaq market-cap quintile (mean cap $31.5bn, median $13.7bn over 2010–2018). Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu define "Large" as above the NYSE median cap. Figures were checked against full text where noted ("verified in full text"). Everything else comes from abstracts or secondary snippets and is flagged as such.

## 1. Closing auction: volume share 2010–2026, noise relative to the pre-close midquote, price discovery and reversal

### Takeaway
US closing auctions grew from about 3% of daily dollar volume in 2010 to about 7.5% in 2018 and about 10% by 2019–2021. Industry sources put a typical 2024 day at about 9%, rising to 20% or more on rebalance and expiry days. For large caps the auction price sits very close to the 4:00 pm midquote: median absolute deviation 1.73 bp, mean 2.66 bp (2010–2018). That gap is mostly the half-spread plus price pressure that reverses almost fully by the next morning (about 85–110%). The auction is therefore a cheap venue for matching volume, but it adds little information. Its price is a slightly noisy, mean-reverting benchmark.

### Cited Findings
**Volume share and its growth**
- Aggregate closing-auction volume was 7.48% of aggregate daily dollar volume in 2018, up from 3.11% in 2010. About $15.2bn traded in the auction on a typical 2018 day. Smith (2006) estimated only 0.49% after Nasdaq introduced its closing cross in 2004. — [Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, June 2021 WP, verified in full text](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- The same paper's Figure 2 (aggregate fraction) goes from "4% in 2010 to 11% in 2018". Auction volume "regularly spikes from the baseline level to about 20% of daily volume". Note that the 7.48% and 11% figures for 2018 differ, probably because of the weighting/volume definition; the paper does not reconcile them in the text I read. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- For the average stock-day, the auction share was 5.69% (mean; median 4.38%) over 2010–2018: 4.13% in 2010 and 7.27% in 2018. The top size quintile had mean 5.67% and median 4.56%. The last five minutes (3:55–4:00) took 6.96% of daily volume (5.79% in 2010, 7.28% in 2018). The 3:30–3:55 window took 10.90% and declined (11.60% to 10.04%). For the top quintile: 3:55–4:00 = 5.83%, 3:30–3:55 = 10.70%. Auction turnover trend is about +10% per year versus about +6.1% per year for pre-close turnover; intraday turnover was roughly flat. Sample: NYSE and Nasdaq common stocks, price > $5, market cap > $100m, Jan 2010–Dec 2018. — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 1 and Section 3.1](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Published version: Journal of Financial Markets 66 (2023) 100852. It was previously circulated as "Should We Use Closing Prices? Institutional Price Pressure at the Close". — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418123000502); [author data page](https://bogousslavsky.github.io/data)
- Closing-auction volume "reached a peak of about 10% of the total trading volume in 2019". — [Jegadeesh & Wu (2022), JFE 143(3):1120–1139, abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v143y2022i3p1120-1139.html)
- In the 2012–2021 sample (NYSE and Nasdaq, about 2,980 stocks), the cross-sectional average auction volume grew "from about 4% ADV at the beginning of the sample period to a peak of about 10% ADV at the end". Mean 7.10% ADV, median 5.34% ADV. Spikes fall on Fridays (option expiry), and the biggest on triple witching. — [Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026), JFQA 61(6):2620–2655, verified in full text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)
- Industry, 2024: "On a typical day in 2024, exchanges matched an average of $50 billion in notional volume during the closing auction, representing roughly 9% of total daily trading volume". On index-rebalance or option-expiry days it was about 20%. On the June 2024 Russell reconstitution day, over 34% of daily notional traded in the auction. — [BMLL (vendor research, not peer-reviewed)](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)
- Industry, Dec 2025: the US allocated 15.39% of daily volume to the closing auction, versus 34.82% in Developed Europe and 16.78% in Asia ex-Japan. Caveat: December contains quad witching and index rebalances, so this is not a typical-day figure. — [State Street Global Advisors](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)
- The NYSE closing auction averaged more than $18bn of notional per day in 2022. — [NYSE, Mar 2023](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/nyse-introduces-closing-auction-imbalance-analysis-tool)
- A Cboe post states that "in October the closing auction made up about 4.5% of total market volume". The year was not visible, and the measure (shares across all securities, including ETFs) probably differs from the stock-level measures above. This shows how sensitive the "auction share" is to its definition. — [Cboe Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/the-impact-closing-auctions-have-on-volumes)

**Who trades at the close**
- A 1% increase in passive mutual fund ownership is associated with a 3.7% increase in auction turnover, but only 0.6% in 3:55–4:00 turnover. Auction turnover rises about 20% permanently after S&P 500 addition and falls about 15% after deletion. Auction and pre-close turnover are 230% and 78% higher on index-rebalancing days, and 87% and 33% higher on month-end days. — [B&M (AEA 2021 version)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7); [B&M JFM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418123000502)
- BlackRock (2020), as cited by B&M, estimates that only about 5% of trading in individual US stocks is attributable to ETF flows. B&M therefore argue for a "liquidity begets liquidity" effect: discretionary traders follow passive flow to the close. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- In the US, ETF creations and redemptions are mostly in kind, so authorised participants need not hedge at the close. The AMF argues this is why passive growth pushed auction share up less in the US than in Europe. — [AMF (France), 2020](https://www.amf-france.org/sites/institutionnel/files/2020-02/growing-importance-of-the-closing-auction-in-share-trading-volumes.pdf)

**Noise of the closing price relative to the 4:00 pm midquote (the 1.7 bp figure, confirmed)**
- Table 4 reports the absolute deviation |log(P_auction / mid_4:00pm)| in bp, 2010–2018. Top size quintile: mean 2.66, SD 3.56, p5 0.45, **median 1.73**, p90 5.69, p99 13.37, p99.9 31.42. Full sample: mean 8.12, median 4.21, p99 63.13. Bottom quintile: mean 20.60, median 12.35. Large ETFs (SPY, QQQ, S&P sector ETFs): mean 3.63 bp, p99 16.32 bp. So the "1.7 bp median for large caps" is confirmed (1.73 bp, June 2021 WP; I did not check whether the published JFM table differs slightly). — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 4 and fn. 11](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- The deviation splits into realised half-spread plus price impact. Full sample: mean half-spread 7.56 bp and mean impact 0.55 bp. Top quintile: half-spread mean 1.47 bp (median 1.12) and impact mean 1.19 bp (median 0.00). 68.5% of auctions print exactly at the pre-close bid or ask, only 1.8% at the midpoint, and 23.4% outside the quotes. The tick size binds for 42% of auctions. — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- End-of-day relative quoted spread, top quintile: mean 2.98 bp, median 2.24 bp (2010–2018). — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 1(b)](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- For large stocks, price deviations trended down over 2010–2018 despite the large rise in auction volume. After the 2016 Tick Size Pilot widened ticks for small stocks, their deviations jumped, which supports a causal tick-size channel. — [B&M 2021 WP, Fig. 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)

**Reversal: overnight and within minutes**
- Overnight reversal: regress log(mid_9:45am,t+1 / P_auction,t) on log(P_auction / mid_4pm). The coefficient is −0.85 for the full sample, so 85% reverses by the next morning. It is about 110% for large stocks and 85% for small stocks. After adjusting for bid-ask bounce: −0.95 overall, −0.97 for large, −0.98 for small. For the top 1% of impact (> 19.70 bp), about 66% reverses overnight. By contrast, only 19% of the last-five-minute (3:55–4:00) return reverses, so the 4 pm midquote move is "mostly efficient". — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 7 and IA.2](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- About half the deviation reverts within 20 minutes after the close, in large stocks with after-hours trades. — [B&M 2021 WP, Table 8](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Price discovery: the auction's weighted price contribution is "about ten times lower than what other periods with similar volume contribute". Adjusted for the spread, it is zero. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- In auction-implied option put-call parity tests, the closing price is "as 'bad' as" the 3:55 pm midquote. — [B&M Dec 2020 version](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- NYSE versus Nasdaq: for large stocks, the NYSE's excess auction deviation is more than a third of the average deviation. The median delay between 4:00 pm and the auction print is 122 s on the NYSE versus 0.2 s on Nasdaq. The authors attribute this to floor-broker D-Quotes, which can be entered until 3:59:50 and are added to the imbalance feed only at 3:55. In an untabulated result the NYSE shows +25 bp excess volatility at the auction relative to Nasdaq. — [B&M 2021 WP, Section 3.4 and fn. 16, 18](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Jegadeesh & Wu: in both exchanges "it takes about 3–5 days for the temporary component of the price impact to fully dissipate", and "trading strategies that exploit this price impact and its reversals are significantly profitable". NYSE auctions offer more depth. — [Jegadeesh & Wu (2022), abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v143y2022i3p1120-1139.html)
- Closing-auction price impact is lower than in the continuous market for all stocks except Nasdaq micro-caps (Jan 2012–Dec 2021, square-root model). Large stocks at 0.5% ADV: NYSE close 3.2 bp versus continuous 6.4 bp; Nasdaq close 7.2 bp versus continuous 8.3 bp. At 5% ADV: NYSE 10.1 versus 20.2 bp; Nasdaq 22.7 versus 26.3 bp. A linear model understates closing impact (2.35 bp versus 17.7 bp at 1% ADV). Auction fees ($0.00085–$0.0027 per share) are below 0.69 bp for the average $39 stock. — [Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026), verified in full text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)

**Earlier and non-US literature on closing calls**
- Cushing & Madhavan (2000), JFM 3(1):45–67, used Russell 1000 transaction data from June 1997 to July 1998 plus all NYSE specialist MOC imbalance indications. The last five minutes explain "almost 18%" of daily return variation in portfolios, but only 4% in individual stocks, while being 1.3% of trading time. They find "systematic return reversals following order imbalance publications consistent with temporary price [pressure]". This is pre-closing-auction-era NYSE and pre-decimalisation. — [Cushing & Madhavan PDF](https://www.chesler.us/resources/academia/trading_at_the_close.pdf); [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeefinmar/v_3a3_3ay_3a2000_3ai_3a1_3ap_3a45-67.htm)
- Pagano & Schwartz (2003), JFE 68(3):439–484, is non-US (Euronext Paris). Adding a closing call lowered execution costs and improved price discovery at the close. — [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222564035_A_closing_call's_impact_on_market_quality_at_Euronext_Paris); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X03000734)
- Kandel, Rindi & Bosetti (2012), J. Financial Intermediation 21:23–49, is non-US (Borsa Italiana). They study how introducing a closing auction affects continuous trading. Paris sets the reference price equal to the auction price. Milan used a VWAP of the last 10% of daily volume, which "reduces the attractiveness of the CA for non-discretionary liquidity traders who must execute at the RP". Paris's auction attracts a larger share of volume. — [KRB PDF](https://didattica.unibocconi.it/mypage/upload/48636_20130115_025154_KRBIMPACTOFCLOSINGCALLAUCTIONJFI2012.PDF)
- B&M summarise the earlier literature: Pagano & Schwartz (2003), Comerton-Forde, Lau & McInish (2007, Singapore), Chelley-Steeley (2008, London), Kandel et al. (2012) and Pagano, Peng & Schwartz (2013, Nasdaq) find that market quality mostly improved when closing auctions were introduced. Bacidore & Lipson (2001) find little benefit from the NYSE closing procedure. — [B&M 2021 WP, literature review](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)

### Inferences
- For S&P 500 names, the official close differs from the 4:00 pm midquote by a typical 1–3 bp. Most of that is half-spread, and it is mean-zero in sign across days. The tail is fat (p99 about 13 bp, p99.9 about 31 bp in 2010–2018), so daily reconciliation against the auction price will occasionally show 10–30 bp gaps that are noise, not execution failure. Most of such a gap reverses by the next morning.
- The deviation reverses about 100% for large caps. So a position marked at the auction price carries a transient mark error that is undone overnight. Day-1 returns measured close-to-close are biased toward the auction imbalance direction, and the bias reverses on day 2.
- Post-2018 evidence (Goyal et al. through 2021; industry through 2025) suggests the auction share kept growing to about 9–10% on normal days. I found no peer-reviewed estimate of the large-cap price-deviation distribution after 2018.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed US auction-share series for 2022–2026; only industry numbers (BMLL, SSGA, NYSE), which use different definitions.
- Cboe's share of closing-auction or closing-price matching volume (Cboe BZX auctions apply only to BZX-listed securities, mostly ETFs) was not found in academic sources.
- The B&M median comes from the June 2021 working paper. I did not check the published JFM 2023 Table 4 for revisions.
- Comerton-Forde et al. (2007) and Pagano & Schwartz (2003) full-text numbers were not extracted.

## 2. Last minutes of continuous trading versus the auction: spreads, depth, volatility, impact. Does a market order at T−60 s pay more?

### Takeaway
The old U-shaped spread (wide at open and close) has given way, for US stocks in the 2010s, to spreads that narrow into the close. Index-fund ownership causally lowers last-five-minute spreads and volatility. A small marketable order in an S&P 500 name in the last minute should therefore pay roughly the end-of-day half-spread (about 1–1.5 bp on average in 2010–2018), not more than mid-day. The main extra "cost" against an official-close benchmark is tracking noise: last-minute price movement plus the auction's own deviation. It is not a worse spread.

### Cited Findings
- Older evidence (1980s–1990s): NYSE spreads were U-shaped or reverse-J-shaped, widest at the open and somewhat wider at the close (McInish & Wood; Brock & Kleidon). Nasdaq dealer-market spreads were stable and narrowed in the final hour. — [Chan, Christie & Schultz (1995)](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~keechung/MGF743/Readings/I5.pdf); [McInish & Wood (Semantic Scholar)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/An-Analysis-of-Intraday-Patterns-in-Bid-Ask-Spreads-Mcinish-Wood/183d5e6de3e6c300a2852112fa5a9923119898ce)
- Recent evidence: "the well-documented U-shaped diurnal pattern of stock liquidity does not hold in recent years: stocks' bid-ask spread appears to decrease, not increase, towards the closing of the trading day." Stocks with high index-fund ownership have higher volume, smaller spreads and lower volatility in the last five minutes relative to their intraday average. Causal identification comes from Russell 1000/2000 switches. — [Jiang & Yao, "U Disappeared: Indexing and the Shift of Diurnal Patterns" (working paper; abstract only)](https://polysz.cuhk.edu.cn/zh-hans/node/12827)
- Upson & Van Ness (2017, JFM 32:49–68) "report that spreads do not follow a U-shape over the trading day anymore, with a lower spread at the close" (as summarised by B&M). — [B&M 2021 WP, Section 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Large-cap end-of-day quoted spread: mean 2.98 bp, median 2.24 bp. Realised half-spread at 4 pm: mean 1.47 bp, median 1.12 bp (top quintile, 2010–2018). — [B&M 2021 WP, Tables 1 and 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Price discovery near the close: weighted price contributions jump when imbalance feeds start (NYSE 3:45 pm, Nasdaq 3:50 pm in their sample), consistent with imbalance information being priced into the continuous market before 4 pm. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Volatility over the last five minutes (1997–98): 4% of individual-stock daily return variance and about 18% of portfolio return variance, while being 1.3% of trading time. This is an outsized common component driven by institutional demand for immediacy. — [Cushing & Madhavan (2000)](https://www.chesler.us/resources/academia/trading_at_the_close.pdf)
- Practitioner view: "Trading at the close is often cost-effective because spreads compress sharply in the final minutes compared to the open". — [SSGA (industry)](https://www.ssga.com/us/en/institutional/insights/how-passive-investing-reshaping-microstructure)
- "Liquidity tends to be high right after open and before close. Spreads are generally lower and market depth higher near the close." — Baltussen et al., quoted in [this X thread](https://x.com/ReformedTrader/status/1487506759514144769) (secondary quote; paper at [Notre Dame](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf))
- Continuous-market price impact in Goyal et al. is estimated over 30-minute intervals. They do not report a separate last-minute estimate. — [Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)
- Cboe reports per-minute volume spikes in the final minutes, with auction-event volume "almost five times greater than continuous trading volumes" for S&P 500 names, and documents the sequence of closing events by exchange. — [Cboe Insights](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/the-impact-closing-auctions-have-on-volumes)

### Inferences
- For a $2.6k–$22k order in an S&P 500 name (far below 0.1% ADV), the square-root impact model gives negligible impact. Scaling Goyal et al.'s 6.4 bp at 0.5% ADV down to 0.001% ADV gives about 0.3 bp. Cost is therefore dominated by the half-spread at T−60 s, which recent evidence says is at or near its daily low.
- Tracking error of a T−60 s fill against the official close has three parts: (a) the last-60 s midquote move, which is mostly efficient and persistent (only 19% of the 3:55–4:00 move reverses); (b) the auction deviation from the 4 pm mid (median 1.7 bp, large caps); (c) the half-spread paid. Part (a) cannot be estimated precisely from these sources. As a rough inference: if the last 5 minutes carry about 4% of daily variance (1997–98) and a large cap has about 150–200 bp daily volatility, one minute is about 15 bp standard deviation. This is an extrapolation from 1997–98 data, not a measured modern figure.
- Market-on-close orders would set this tracking error to zero by construction. Their cost is the auction fee (< 0.7 bp) and exposure to the auction's own deviation, which is precisely the benchmark.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed paper measuring effective spreads and price impact minute by minute in the final 1–10 minutes for S&P 500 stocks after 2018, nor one comparing a T−60 s market order with an MOC fill. Jiang & Yao report last-5-minute versus intraday-average comparisons, but I saw only the abstract (no numbers).
- No source quantified last-minute midquote volatility for large caps in the 2020s.
- Full-text numbers from Upson & Van Ness (2017) were not extracted.

## 3. Intraday U-shapes and systematic end-of-day price pressure

### Takeaway
Several robust patterns concentrate at the end of the day:
- the cross-section repeats its half-hour returns at daily lags (Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka);
- the market's last half-hour continues the day's move (Gao et al.; Baltussen et al.). This is linked to short-gamma and leveraged-ETF hedging and partially reverses over the next days;
- mispricing and anomaly portfolios do badly in the last half-hour because arbitrageurs unwind before the overnight period, and small and illiquid stocks earn their premium then (Bogousslavsky).

Taken together, a close trader's fill price includes predictable, partly transitory end-of-day pressure. The sign depends on the day's market move and on what the book holds.

### Cited Findings
- **Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010), JF 65:1369–1407.** They find "a striking pattern of return continuation at half-hour intervals that are exact multiples of a trading day", lasting at least 40 trading days. Volume, order imbalance, volatility and spreads show similar periodicity but do not explain it. Short-term reversal is driven by temporary liquidity imbalances lasting under an hour and by bid-ask bounce. "Timing trades can reduce execution costs by the equivalent of the effective spread." The pattern is not tied to size, S&P 500 inclusion, day of week or turn of month. — [HKS (JF version PDF)](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka-jf-2010-01-07.pdf)
- **Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (2018), JFE "Market intraday momentum".** Data: SPY, Feb 1993–Dec 2013. The first half-hour return (from the previous close to 10:00) predicts the last half-hour return: slope 6.94 (×100), R² 1.6%. With the 12th half-hour added, R² is 2.6%. Out-of-sample R² is 1.4% and 2.0%. Predictability is stronger on high-volatility, high-volume, recession and macro-news days, and holds for ten other ETFs. Timing strategy: 6.67% per year, SD 6.19%, Sharpe 1.08, versus buy-and-hold 6.04% per year, SD 20.57%, Sharpe 0.29. After transaction costs the timing strategy earns 4.46% per year. "Always long" in the last half-hour earned −0.74% per year in that table. — [Gao et al. WP, verified in full text excerpts](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/ee7dac49-530b-4950-b5d0-e0b5eee08f2e.pdf); [JFE](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X18301351)
- **Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021), JFE 142:377–403.** Data: more than 60 futures on equities, bonds, commodities and currencies, 1974–2020. The last-30-minute return is predicted by the rest-of-day return (previous close to 30 minutes before close). This "reverts over the next days" and is linked to gamma hedging by option market makers and leveraged ETFs. For the S&P 500 index (16:00 close, Apr 1982–May 2020): β_ROD = 5.98 (×100, t = 4.78), R² 3.28%. For S&P 500 futures (Jan 1996–May 2020): β_ROD = 5.08 (t = 4.72). Cheng & Madhavan (2010), cited in the paper, estimated that LETF rebalancing made up 16.8% (50.2%) of market-on-close volume on a day the market moved 1% (5%), as of Feb 2009. Shum et al. (2015) note that LETF hedging can start 30 minutes before the close because of MOC fill risk. — [Baltussen et al. PDF](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Tinbergen abstract](https://tinbergen.nl/publication/168044/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum)
- Baltussen et al. (as quoted): "Intraday momentum is much more pronounced on negative NGE [net gamma exposure] days. On days with positive NGE, however, there is no significant intraday momentum." — [secondary quote in X thread](https://x.com/ReformedTrader/status/1487506759514144769)
- **Bogousslavsky (2021), JFE 141(1):172–194.** "Margin requirements are higher overnight, and lending fees are typically charged only on positions held overnight… arbitrageurs who trade on mispricing [reduce] their positions before the end of the day… a mispricing factor earns positive returns throughout the day but performs poorly at the end of the day. This pattern strengthens in the second half of the sample and is shared by several well-known anomalies." — [IDEAS abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i1p172-194.html)
- Job-market version of the same paper (thirty-year US sample): "Size and illiquidity premia are realized in the last thirty minutes of trading". Small-stock turnover relative to large-stock turnover spikes at the close. Last-half-hour returns rise monotonically with size and first-half-hour returns fall with size. Profitability and idiosyncratic-volatility anomalies accrue during the day but suffer large negative overnight returns. — [Bogousslavsky JMP 2016](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/The%2520Cross-Section%2520of%2520Intraday%2520and%2520Overnight%2520Returns_21Dec1620170112151545.pdf)
- Auction-level evidence: closing-auction deviations, which are price pressure, reverse about 85–110% overnight, versus 19% of the 3:55–4:00 move. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Momentum effect at the close.** On trending days (especially with negative dealer gamma), market-level momentum into the close means a long/short book trading at T−60 s fills after part of the day's continuation has already happened.
  - The market-level effect largely nets out across longs and shorts and the SPY hedge.
  - It does not net out on the hedge leg itself, or if the book is net long or short.
  - The partial multi-day reversal means close-to-close returns measured from the close carry a small transient component.
- **Mispricing-anomaly books.** If an LLM news signal behaves like a mispricing signal (longs underpriced, shorts overpriced), Bogousslavsky's result implies such stocks tend to move against the signal in the last half-hour.
  - Entering at the close may therefore get a slightly better entry price than mid-day.
  - The position then earns or loses the overnight component.
  - This is a hypothesis to test on the book's own fills, not an established fact for news signals.
- The Gao et al. effect was strongest in 1993–2013 and on high-volatility days. I found no post-2020 out-of-sample update in this search.

### Gaps
- HKS's exact sample period was not extracted (I believe it is TAQ-era NYSE, but did not verify).
- No figures were extracted for the size of the last-half-hour mispricing-factor return in the published Bogousslavsky (2021) paper (bp per day), or for its sample period.
- No post-2020 evidence was found on whether market intraday momentum persisted amid 0DTE options growth (2022 onward). This is an important gap for gamma-related end-of-day pressure.

## 4. Open versus close: cost and benefit of trading at the open versus the close

### Takeaway
The open is the expensive, information-heavy end of the day:
- opening auctions are small (median 0.92% ADV) and have much higher price impact than closing auctions;
- liquidity at the open (effective spread, depth) deteriorated in 2010–2018 as volume moved to the close;
- opening prices of attention-grabbing stocks are systematically too high and reverse during the day.

US equity-premium and momentum returns accrue largely overnight, while many anomaly returns accrue intraday. For a daily-rebalanced long/short book, trading at the close is cheaper, and it captures the overnight return of positions just entered.

### Cited Findings
- **Opening auctions are illiquid.** Over 2012–2021, average opening-auction volume was 1.58% ADV (median 0.92%); for large stocks the mean was 1.36% and median 0.84%. "The price impact in opening auctions is much larger than in closing auctions." Opening auctions "are likely to attract informed traders" because of overnight news. "It is unlikely that the opening auction would be an attractive mechanism for traders whose goal is to minimize the price impact of their trades." — [Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026), Table 4 and Section III.B](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)
- **Liquidity at the open got worse as volume moved to the close.** Sample: 333 large stocks, 92% of them S&P 500, 2010–2018, measured over 9:30–9:45. Turnover at the open fell about 21%. Effective spread rose about 10 bp ("substantial for S&P 500 stocks"). Depth at the best quotes fell about 63%. The weighted price contribution of the first 15 minutes rose by 3–4 percentage points (about 20%). — [B&M 2021 WP, Section 5](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- **Berkman, Koch, Tuttle & Zhang (2012), JFQA 47(4):715–741.** There is "a strong tendency for positive returns during the overnight period followed by reversals during the trading day… driven by an opening price that is high relative to intraday prices". The effect is concentrated in stocks that recently attracted retail attention, is larger for hard-to-value or costly-to-arbitrage stocks, and is larger in high-sentiment periods. "The additional implicit transaction costs for retail traders who buy high-attention stocks near the open frequently exceed the effective half spread." — [IDEAS abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v47y2012i04p715-741_00.html)
- **Cliff, Cooper & Gulen (2008, SSRN WP), "Like Night and Day".** "The US equity premium over the last decade is solely due to overnight returns." Day returns are "close to zero and sometimes negative". The effect holds for stocks, indexes and index futures on NYSE and Nasdaq. It is "driven in part by high opening prices which subsequently decline in the first hour of trading". — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1004081)
- **Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019), JFE 134:192–213.** Sample 1993–2013, excluding microcaps. Momentum profits accrue entirely overnight: three-factor alpha 0.953%/month overnight (t = 3.65) versus 0.113% intraday (t = 0.27), out of 1.053% total. Profits on other strategies (size, value, profitability and others) accrue intraday, with a partly offsetting overnight premium of the opposite sign. Institutions "prefer to trade during the day and against the momentum characteristic". Conditional variation in overnight-minus-intraday momentum returns is about 2% per month. Results are consistent in nine non-US markets. — [FMG DP744](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/publications/DP744.pdf); [JFE version PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf); [NBER 2015 draft](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2015/APf15/Lou_Polk_Skouras.pdf)
- **Hendershott, Livdan & Rösch (2020), JFE 138(3):635–662.** "The CAPM holds overnight when the market is closed." Open-to-close returns are negatively related to beta in the cross-section. The result holds for beta-sorted, industry and book-to-market portfolios, in the US and internationally. — [HLR WP PDF](https://jhfinance.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/12369/2018/11/2019JH20180821_Asset-Pricing-A-Tale-of-Night-and-Day.pdf)
- Kelly & Clark (2011), "Returns in trading versus non-trading hours: the difference is day and night", J. Asset Management 12:132–145. Cited in Lou, Polk & Skouras; the full text was not reviewed. — [LPS reference list](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)
- Bogousslavsky (JMP version): overnight returns on aggregate portfolios "are large, but their magnitude is sensitive to the definition of the opening price". — [Bogousslavsky JMP](https://finance.unibocconi.eu/sites/default/files/files/media/attachments/The%2520Cross-Section%2520of%2520Intraday%2520and%2520Overnight%2520Returns_21Dec1620170112151545.pdf)
- B&M use the 9:45 midquote rather than the open "to avoid noisy and unreliable quotes over the first minutes of trading". — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Execution cost.** For a daily-rebalanced S&P 500 long/short book with small orders, the literature points to the close as cheaper than the open:
  - lower and narrowing spreads into the close;
  - the cheapest auction mechanism;
  - the open's widening effective spreads (about +10 bp over 2010–2018 at 9:30–9:45 for large stocks) and illiquid opening auctions.
- **Return capture.** A signal formed at the close and executed at the next open forgoes the overnight return. Overnight is where the market premium, momentum and beta premia concentrate (Cliff et al.; Lou et al.; Hendershott et al.). Entering at the same day's close captures it. If the signal relates to news that retail attention also chases, buying at the next open risks paying the Berkman et al. inflated opening price.
- **The flip side.** Signals of the "mispricing/anomaly" type earn intraday (Lou et al.; Bogousslavsky). For those, entering at the close and exiting at a close holds through the adverse overnight component. The best entry time is therefore signal-dependent.

### Gaps
- No study found directly simulates "trade at the open versus at the close" for a daily long/short book with costs, in bp per year. The pieces above have to be combined.
- Berkman et al.'s sample period and exact cost in bp were not extracted (abstract only).
- Kelly & Clark (2011) content and sample were not verified.
- The overnight-premium literature mostly ends by 2013–2018. I did not verify post-2020 persistence; extended-hours and retail activity changed market structure after 2020.

## 5. Optimal execution theory for a tiny order, and the official close as benchmark

### Takeaway
Classical models (Bertsimas–Lo, Almgren–Chriss) justify splitting orders only when the order is large relative to liquidity; equal splitting is optimal only in a special case. For orders that are tiny relative to ADV, impact is negligible and the problem is dominated by spread and risk relative to the benchmark. When the benchmark is the official close, the model results agree: put (almost) everything into the closing auction. Trade in the continuous market only when the order is big enough to move the auction, and then before the imbalance feed starts.

### Cited Findings
- **Bertsimas & Lo (1998), JFM 1:1–50.** Dynamic programming for minimum expected cost of executing a block over T periods. "The common approach of breaking up a large trade into a number of smaller trades of equal size is optimal only in one very special case: when price impact is linear in the trade size, permanent in its effect on future prices, and when prices follow an arithmetic random walk." — [Bertsimas & Lo PDF](https://www.mit.edu/~dbertsim/papers/Finance/Optimal%20control%20of%20execution%20costs.pdf)
- **Almgren & Chriss (2001), J. Risk 3:5–40.** They target the arrival-price benchmark with explicit risk aversion (mean-variance trade-off between impact and timing risk); described as such in [Frei et al. (NASDAQ close note)](https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~cfrei/CFCY2018.pdf).
- **Target Close in the Almgren–Chriss framework (Labadie & Lehalle).** "Since the benchmark… is the closing price, the trader has interest in executing most of their order at the close auction. However, if the number of shares to trade is too large, the order cannot be totally executed at the close auction without moving the price too much… Therefore, the trader has to trade some shares during the continuous auction phase." They derive optimal start times for Target Close given a minimum trading size. — [arXiv 1205.3482](https://arxiv.org/html/1205.3482v6)
- **Frei et al., NASDAQ close.** In a mean-variance model with the closing price as benchmark, "it is not beneficial for the investor to trade after the imbalance announcement". The optimal strategy "puts a major part into the closing auction and smaller, exponentially increasing fractions in the open market before the imbalance announcement". If the trader's order has no impact on the auction price (α = 0), it is "optimal to trade only in the closing auction". — [Frei et al., "An Explicit Optimal Strategy for Flow Trades at NASDAQ around the Close"](https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~cfrei/CFCY2018.pdf)
- **Frei & Westray (2018), Quantitative Finance 18:655–671 (non-US, Hong Kong).** The Hong Kong close was then the median of five prices in the last minute. Even optimal strategies only slightly beat an average trader. The difficulty comes from benchmark risk and execution risk, "mainly a consequence of the trader's inability to trade at the exact price used in the benchmark calculation, combined with the occurrence of wide spreads". — [Frei & Westray PDF](https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~cfrei/CFNW2018.pdf)
- **Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026).** Price impact follows a square-root law in auctions and in the continuous market (median exponent 0.45 in the continuous market). In theory traders "would allocate trades across mechanisms to equalize marginal price impact". Annual trading costs for long/short anomaly portfolios placed in the cheaper mechanism are 17–41 bp, or 9–21 bp in closing auctions excluding microcaps. — [Goyal et al. full text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)
- **Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010).** Because of intraday periodicity, "timing trades can reduce execution costs by the equivalent of the effective spread." — [HKS](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/departments/finance/documents/Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka-jf-2010-01-07.pdf)

### Inferences
- For orders of about 0.001%–0.01% ADV, the theory says:
  - no splitting is needed, since square-root impact is about 0.1–0.5 bp;
  - TWAP or VWAP over the day would add benchmark risk against the close (intraday volatility) for no impact saving;
  - a limit order risks non-fill at the close.
- Against an official-close benchmark, a single MOC order is the benchmark-tracking-optimal choice (α ≈ 0 in Frei et al.). A single marketable order at T−60 s is a close approximation: it pays about a half-spread, plus last-minute and auction-deviation tracking noise that is mean-zero but non-zero in variance.
- The model results say not to trade after the imbalance publication if you have impact. A tiny order has none, so trading at T−60 s carries no meaningful information-leakage cost. It only bears the variance described in section 2.

### Gaps
- I found no academic paper explicitly comparing "single market order at T−1 min" with "MOC" in tracking error and cost for small orders in US large caps.
- The Almgren–Chriss original PDF was not fetched; its content is cited via secondary description.
- The author list of the Frei NASDAQ note (file CFCY2018) was not confirmed beyond Christoph Frei.

## 6. MOC orders, imbalance publications (15:50/15:55) and late-day predictability

### Takeaway
Exchange imbalance feeds (NYSE from 3:45, moved to 3:50 in 2019; Nasdaq 3:50, briefly 3:55 in 2018–19) carry information that moves continuous-market prices before 4 pm. The NYSE feed was historically less accurate because floor-broker D-orders were added only at 3:55. Imbalance-driven price pressure at the close reverses over hours to days, and reversal strategies based on MOC imbalances were profitable in academic samples (1997–98; 2010s).

### Cited Findings
- **Timeline.** The NYSE started disseminating closing information at 3:45 pm and switched to 3:50 pm on 1 Apr 2019. Nasdaq disseminated from 3:50 pm, switched to 3:55 pm on 29 Oct 2018, and returned to 3:50 pm on 15 Apr 2019. Nasdaq Imbalance-Only orders and NYSE Closing Offset orders are accepted until 3:58 pm. — [Goyal, Jegadeesh & Wu (2026), fn. 5–7](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846/S0022109026102592a.pdf/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies.pdf)
- In B&M's sample, MOC and LOC orders had to be entered before 3:45 pm unless offsetting a regulatory imbalance. NYSE floor-broker D-Quotes could be entered or modified until 3:59:50 and were included in the imbalance feed only from 3:55. The NYSE feed is published every five seconds. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf); [B&M AEA version](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)
- **Jegadeesh & Wu (2022).** Nasdaq imbalances "drop by about 80% immediately after the start of closing information dissemination". NYSE imbalances "remain virtually constant until 3:55 p.m., then they drop sharply when closing information starts including orders placed through floor brokers". Floor-broker orders are about 20% of NYSE closing auction volume. New orders after first dissemination are only about 0.1% of Nasdaq auction volume. — [Jegadeesh & Wu, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21005092)
- B&M report that "Wu and Jegadeesh (2020) argue that reversal strategies based on market-on-close order imbalances are profitable". Jegadeesh & Wu's published abstract adds that temporary impact takes 3–5 days to dissipate. — [B&M AEA version](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7); [Jegadeesh & Wu abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v143y2022i3p1120-1139.html)
- Hu & Murphy ("Vestigial Tails? Floor Brokers at the Close in Modern Electronic Markets", working paper) find NYSE imbalance information less precise than Nasdaq's because D-Quotes are excluded until 3:55. They also find auction quality "substantially worse for NYSE stocks". This is as summarised by B&M; I did not review the full text. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- Weighted price contributions for NYSE (Nasdaq) stocks jump at 3:45 (3:50), consistent with imbalance information being incorporated into prices. — [B&M 2021 WP](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/6310c0b9bb63a25599f4418c/t/634ffc92f81e226b2c30654f/1666186387645/who-trades-at-the-close_June2021.pdf)
- **Cushing & Madhavan (2000).** Using all NYSE MOC imbalance indications for June 1997–July 1998, they find "systematic return reversals following order imbalance publications consistent with temporary price [pressure]". — [Cushing & Madhavan](https://www.chesler.us/resources/academia/trading_at_the_close.pdf)
- A master's thesis (Imperial College, not peer-reviewed) regresses forward returns in the last 10 minutes on imbalance-feed data. Predictability for S&P 500 stocks on Nasdaq is "good, and increasing when we approach MOC and LOC cut-off time", and the model can "earn 30% of the spread". It reports that S&P 500 auction volume rose from about 4% to above 10% of daily volume over about eight years, and that the last half-hour is more than 25% of daily volume. — [Morand, Imperial MSc thesis](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/faculty-of-natural-sciences/department-of-mathematics/math-finance/MORAND_CLEA_01805978.pdf)
- **NYSE (industry, 2025).** In NYSE closing auctions since the start of 2025, marketable "untapped" liquidity at the closing price averages about 3.3% of executed auction volume, and over 6.3% for non-Russell-1000 names. More than a quarter of auctions have at least 2% of executed volume cancelled back for lack of contra interest. — [NYSE Insights](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/behind-the-record-volumes-a-hidden-opportunity)
- **Floor closure.** Hu & Murphy (2021) and Jegadeesh & Wu (2022) examine how the 2020 NYSE floor closure (COVID) affected closing-auction liquidity. Only the reference was found, not the results. — [ScienceDirect snippet](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X21005092)

### Inferences
- A trader who sends a marketable order at T−60 s (3:59 pm) acts after the imbalance feed has run for 4–9 minutes. Part of the imbalance-induced move is already in the continuous price, which is mostly efficient, and the remainder shows up as the auction deviation, which reverses. Imbalance information could in principle be used to choose between continuous execution and the auction, or to time the order. For a book this size the expected gain is at most a fraction of a basis point to a few bp per trade, and only in the direction of the imbalance.
- On NYSE-listed names the auction print comes about 2 minutes after 4 pm (2010–2018 median) and carries floor-broker uncertainty. Reconciliation noise against the official close is therefore somewhat larger for NYSE listings than for Nasdaq listings.

### Gaps
- Full-text quantitative results from Jegadeesh & Wu (2022): reversal-strategy returns in bp, sample period, and the floor-closure effect. Only the abstract and snippets were available.
- Hu & Murphy's full results and publication status were not verified.
- I found no peer-reviewed post-2020 study of imbalance-feed predictability for S&P 500 names. The only direct evidence is a master's thesis and industry notes.
- Current (2026) MOC/LOC cut-off times per exchange were not verified in this search beyond the 2012–2021 timeline above.
